A large part of my daily work in GCMS is identity verification and data integrity, whether I'm resolving a duplicate UCI, an unlinked profile, or a biometric record applied to the wrong file.

Before I execute a merge, an association, or a disassociation, I work through every data point on the file. I check the biographic data (names with transliterations, dates and places of birth, parents, gender, nationality), the travel documents in the main documents tab (passport number, issuing country, dates of issue and expiry, prior passports on record), the biometric records under the criminality tab (10-print fingerprints, facial biometrics, match scores), the application history (prior applications, refusals, family and household linkages), and the supporting documents in the eDocs tab (national IDs, birth and marriage certificates). The data has to triangulate across all of these layers. If it does, the file represents a single identity and the action proceeds. If a name variant won't reconcile, a date of birth shifts between applications, a passport number appears on two profiles, or biometrics don't match the claimed identity, the discrepancy gets resolved before I move.

Passports themselves are often the anchor in that analysis. They carry biographic data, photographs over time, issuing-state details, and serial numbers that either confirm a single identity or expose a gap that needs to be explained. Reviewing passport applications, assessing supporting documentation, and making entitlement decisions in GCMS is the same analytical work applied to a different statute, the Canadian Passport Order rather than the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act. I apply IRPA and IRCC policy across the units I've worked in: BOSU on biometric operational support spanning CIBIDS, biometric technology, and biometric and biographic associations and disassociations; CISU on potential data privacy breaches; and DI on identity record corrections, UCI merges, and household work including PPMI and IMM. Each decision is documented in GCMS so the file stays audit-ready and the reasoning defensible.
